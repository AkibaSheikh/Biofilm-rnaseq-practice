Untreated biofilm and planktonic cells of P. aeruginosa differed widely in gene expression: 1,489 genes were higher in biofilm and 1,254 were higher in planktonic (adjusted p < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1, n = 3 per group), and PC1 explained 98% of the variance. Among the 30 most significant genes, 22 were higher in biofilm and 8 were higher in planktonic, including genes annotated as the fucose-binding lectin LecB, a chemotaxis transducer and the potassium-transporting ATPase subunits KdpA and KdpB (biofilm), and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase and a cytochrome c oxidase subunit (planktonic). KEGG enrichment of the genes higher in biofilm highlighted two-component systems, bacterial chemotaxis and flagellar assembly, whereas genes higher in planktonic were enriched in the ribosome, oxidative phosphorylation, carbon metabolism and the TCA cycle. The data show association, not mechanism. This raises the question of whether the lower expression of protein-synthesis and energy-metabolism genes in biofilm cells reflects slower growth, and how that relates to stress tolerance.
